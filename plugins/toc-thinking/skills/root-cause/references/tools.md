# The tools, as published

The structure of each Thinking Process diagram, with the wording of the
sources it was checked against. `sources.md` says where each source was read
and which could not be. Quotations marked TOCICO are from the *TOCICO
Dictionary*, second edition. Quotations marked Dettmer are from *The Logical
Thinking Process* (2007).

## Two logics

TOCICO, *sufficiency-based logic*: "A type of cause-and-effect reasoning in
which the existence of one or more causes leads unavoidably to the existence
of the effect." Read from the tail of the arrow: "If ... then ...". Causes
that are needed together are joined by "a logical 'and' connector (or
ellipse)". The diagrams that use it: current reality tree, future reality
tree, negative branch reservation, transition tree, strategy and tactics
tree.

TOCICO, *necessity-based logic*: "A type of logic in which each entity at the
tail of an arrow must exist in order for the entity at the head of the arrow
to exist." Read from the head, in order to have B we must have D. The
diagrams that use it: evaporating cloud and prerequisite tree. "The validity
of the connections is scrutinized by questioning the validity of the
assumptions underlying the relationship between the two entities."

## The set of tools

TOCICO's *thinking processes* entry lists eight: "current reality tree
(branch, twig), evaporating cloud, future reality tree (branch, twig),
negative branch reservation, prerequisite tree, intermediate objectives map,
transition tree, and strategy and tactics tree." Goldratt's original set was
five trees for three questions (Dettmer, chapter 7). This skill uses the
original five and the negative branch reservation.

## Undesirable effect

TOCICO's *undesirable effect* entry lists the properties of a well-stated UDE.
Among them: a complete sentence, present tense, a state and not an action, a
single entity, factual, and not a speculated cause. The entry gives no count.
Dettmer, chapter 4: "you can start a CRT with as few as one."

## Current Reality Tree

TOCICO: "A thinking processes sufficiency-based logic diagram that
facilitates answering the question ... 'what to change?' by illustrating the
cause and effect relationships between the core problem and the undesirable
effects (UDEs) in a system."

Two terms at the bottom of the tree:

- *root cause* (TOCICO): "An entity usually appearing at the lowest level of
  causality within a current reality tree for which there are no postulated
  causes." Several can exist.
- *core problem* (TOCICO): "There may be one or more core problems, however,
  generally one to three core problems will account for over 70% of the UDEs
  in an environment." The *root cause* entry adds: "If a root cause results
  in the majority of the UDEs including the most significant ones, it may
  be labeled as the core problem."

Loops: Dettmer, chapter 4, describes the negative reinforcing loop, an effect
that "actually reinforces the cause that produced it", and says that
"eliminating the critical root cause that produces a negative reinforcing
loop is one of the most powerful changes for good that you can make."

## Evaporating Cloud

TOCICO: "A cloud is composed of an objective A, two requirements (B and C)
that must be met to achieve A and two prerequisites (D and D') that express
the conflict." Synonyms in the same dictionary: conflict cloud, conflict
diagram, conflict resolution diagram. Requirements are needs and
prerequisites are wants: "the existence of a prerequisite to satisfy a
requirement is analogous to a want existing to satisfy a need."

TOCICO gives the two steps: "1. Constructing a logically sound cloud (the
five box diagram) and all the supportive arguments (assumptions) to explain
its existence in reality; and 2. Developing a solution by selecting
injections that can negate an assumption and cause the conflict to
disappear. That is the reason for using the term 'evaporating'."

The five arrows carry the assumptions: A-B, A-C, B-D, C-D' and D-D'
(TOCICO, *three-cloud approach*). An injection (TOCICO) is "a state,
condition, or action that invalidates one or more assumptions underlying
the relationships" on any of them. Dettmer, chapter 5: "All Arrows Are Fair
Game."

## Future Reality Tree and Negative Branch Reservation

TOCICO, *future reality tree*: "A thinking processes sufficiency-based logic
diagram that facilitates answering the question ... 'To what to change?' by
presenting a sequence of cause-and-effect relationships that links proposed
injection(s) to desired effects (DEs)." Its two purposes: to show that the
injections "will cause the majority of the existing UDEs in a system to be
replaced by DEs", and to "identify and prevent from occurring any new
problems or UDEs", which "is achieved through the negative branch
reservation process."

TOCICO, *negative branch reservation*: "An unintended negative consequence
(i.e., new undesirable effect) of a proposed problem solution or injection."
"NBRs require a secondary or trimming injection at the point where the tree
branch starts turning negative ... The process of adding a trimming
injection is called 'trimming the negative branch.'"

## Prerequisite Tree

TOCICO: "A necessity-based logic diagram that facilitates answering the
question ... 'how to cause the change?' by showing the relationships between
an injection or ambitious target to be implemented, obstacles that block the
implementation, and the intermediate objectives that overcome the
obstacles." It "shows the sequence in which the intermediate objectives must
be achieved".

- *obstacle* (TOCICO): "Something that blocks or prevents a desirable system
  condition, state of being, or desired action from occurring. ...
  Overcoming an obstacle requires the identification and achievement of an
  intermediate objective."
- *intermediate objective* (TOCICO): "A transitional condition or milestone
  that must be achieved before a main objective can be attained."

## Transition Tree

TOCICO: "A thinking processes sufficiency-based logic diagram that
facilitates answering the question ... 'how to cause the change?' by
providing a detailed, step-by-step set of actions". It "is formed by
expanding the elements identified in the prerequisite tree into a more
detailed set of needs, current states and future states or intermediate
objectives expected, and actions required along with their respective
rationales".

Dettmer, chapter 7, on the element count: "The repeating structure of the
original TT had only four elements", existing reality, need, action and
expected effect. "With the addition of a fifth", the rationale for the
action, "Goldratt thought the 'why do this?' question would be answered."
The same chapter records that the 2007 book omits instructions for building
the tree, in favour of a detailed prerequisite tree and project management.
This skill keeps the five-element tree because a software change needs the
step-level plan.

## Strategy and Tactics Tree

TOCICO: "A thinking process (a logic tree) to define and communicate all the
necessary and sufficient changes required to synchronize actions for the
achievement of any ambitious target". It is outside this skill's procedure.
